# Copy

The user usually provides initial copy. Your job: keep their meaning and
facts, sharpen the words, and fit them to the design. Never invent facts.

## Headlines

- **Outcome over category.** "Smarter checking that finds the money you're
  missing" beats "AI-powered banking platform".
- **Specific beats clever.** Numbers, names, timeframes: "Protected from day
  one", "$25 every payday", "five minutes".
- **Short.** Hero 6–14 words. Section titles 3–9 words. One idea each.
- **Rhythm.** Two-beat lines land well: "Most banks pay you once. Topper keeps going."
- **Accent phrase** = the emotional payload ("protect you", "the money you're
  missing"). Mark it with `<em>` or `<Mark>`.
- Avoid: "Unlock", "Unleash", "Elevate", "Empower", "Supercharge",
  "Revolutionize", "Seamless", "Cutting-edge", "Next-generation",
  "In today's fast-paced world", "Look no further", rhetorical questions as
  headlines, puns that need explaining.

## Body

- Lead (subhead): how the promise is kept, 1–2 sentences, ≤ 35 words.
- Section copy: 1–3 short sentences. If it needs more, it's two sections or
  an FAQ.
- Second person, active voice, present tense. Contractions are fine.
- Concrete nouns and verbs. Cut adverbs and hedges ("very", "really", "simply").
- Feature names only if the user knows them; otherwise describe the benefit
  and name it second ("Payday Boost: $25 every time your paycheck lands").

## CTAs

- Verb + outcome, 2–4 words: "Start switching", "Find my edge", "Open Safe
  Start Checking", "Book a 20-minute call".
- The same primary CTA label everywhere (consistency builds momentum).
- Reassurance microcopy right beside it: "No monthly fees", "About 5
  minutes", "No credit check", "Cancel anytime". Only if true.

## Microcopy

- Form labels are nouns ("Work email"), errors say how to fix ("Enter an
  email like name@company.com"), success says what happens next and when.
- Empty, loading and error states have real words.
- Numbers formatted consistently (`$3,240.00` vs `$3,240`: choose one per context).

## Proof and claims

- Every claim must trace to the brief or the user. Keep numbers in
  `site.config.ts` `facts` and reference them from copy and artifacts.
- Regulated categories (banking, lending, investing, insurance, health,
  legal): required disclosures in the footer `legal` array; mark
  illustrative figures ("Rewards shown are illustrative…"); don't promise
  outcomes. Name the issuing bank if the brief says it's a fintech
  partnership. Ask the user when unsure.
- No fake testimonials, logos, user counts, ratings, awards, press.

## Editing pass checklist

For every line on the page:
1. Does it say something the visitor cares about?
2. Could a competitor put it on their site unchanged? (If yes, sharpen.)
3. Can it lose a third of its words?
4. Is every fact sourced?
5. Does it read naturally aloud?
